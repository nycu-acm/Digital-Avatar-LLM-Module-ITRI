Service Description
When a crack grows, the energy required to generate a new crack surface is called surface energy, which can be obtained through experimental measurements.The value of surface energy is an important basis for building rupture judgments and is closely related to the fracture toughness or critical value of the rupture parameters of the material.The Institute of Technology provides the following instrument measurement services:
Services
Surface Energy Analyzer (SEA-IGC).
Surface Energy Analyzer (SEA-IGC)
That is, inverse gas chromatography (IGC), which measures the surface characteristics of powders, particles, fibers, and semi-solids, and is described by surface energy. It is a solid-phase surface characteristic analysis instrument. It is like the principle of gas-phase layer absorption. The probe solvent is vaporized into the pipe column and passes through the surface of the sample to be tested.It has different residence times due to the influence of the intermolecular forces between solid and gas.A series of measurement data is collected and calculated through theoretical formulas to analyze and obtain the solid surface energy and acid-base characteristics.Probe molecules used:
Non-polarity: n-hexane, n-heptane, n-octane.
Polar molecules: ethanol, chloroform, dichloromethane, toluene, ethyl acetate.
