In order to solve the problem of blind spots in sight, the Industrial Research Institute has developed the iRoadSafe smart road safety warning system. On the 4th, the US time was awarded the Edison Awards, which is known as the "Oscar for Innovation" and won this honor together with internationally renowned institutions such as Dow Chemical, Adobe, Abbott, 3M, IBM, etc., allowing Taiwan's innovative ICT technology to shine around the world.
With the support of the Science and Technology Project of the Ministry of Economic Affairs, the "iRoadSafe Smart Road Safety Warning System" of the Institute of Industrial Research won the favor of the jury of the 2019 Edison Award and was recognized by the "Transportation and Logistics-Passenger Safety".The iRoadSafe smart road safety warning system uses DSRC workshop communication equipment developed by the Institute of Technology and Research, and has also been certified by the U.S. Department of Transportation rQPL (Research Qualified Products List).
Frank, executive director of the Edison Award. Frank Bonafilia praised the iRoadSafe smart road safety warning system that won the award by the Institute of Technology.He said that the population of modern cities is increasing, and the rapid development towards giants will inevitably generate a large amount of information.Therefore, he believes that the iRoadSafe system can not only provide a safer traffic environment for passers-by, master the interaction between people, cars and roads, but also lead people's future lifestyles.
Representatives of the iRoadSafe smart road safety warning system team of the Institute of Technology Research, also visited Dr. Mohamad Talas, deputy director of the New York City Transportation Bureau system engineering transportation operations on the afternoon of the second day of the United States, to exchange on the current technology development and practical application results of the Internet of Vehicles.Dr. Talas, who is mainly responsible for the smart transportation system, is deeply interested in the field verification experience of the Institute of Technology Research, such as research on precise positioning with short-distance wireless communication technology (DSRC). The New York City Internet of Vehicles Demonstration Project began in 2015 and is about to enter the final results evaluation stage. The smart road safety warning system developed by the Institute of Technology has also been verified in many cities in Taiwan.The two sides have heated discussions on the application and challenges of many in-vehicle communication technologies.
Que Zhike, director of the Information and Communications Research Institute of the Institute of Industrial Research, said that the Internet of Vehicles has become more and more mature in recent years.All countries are working hard to develop workshop communications and have successively legislated legislation to promote the application and services of the Internet of Vehicles, indicating that future communication technology can enter the old car market, roadside construction, application services and other Internet of Vehicles business opportunities.The Institute of Technology hopes to work with the industry to realize smart intersections and smart cities through information and communication technology, so that Taiwan can enter the blue ocean market and enhance its international competitiveness.
my country suffers economic losses of up to NT$450 billion in traffic accidents every year. Nearly 60% of traffic accidents occur at intersections. Most of them are caused by vehicles passing through Wuhaozhi Road and failing to pay attention to the intersection status or rushing to the intersection of Wuhaozhi Road or grabbing red or yellow lights at intersections. It is also the most serious casualties between vehicles and pedestrians.Therefore, how to reduce intersection collision accidents and improve intersection safety is an urgent issue at present.
Que Zhike further pointed out that generally speaking, when a vehicle is anti-collision, all vehicles must be equipped with the same technology to calculate the relative distance through communication. However, it is impossible for every vehicle to be equipped with similar technology at present. Therefore, in order to make up for this dilemma, the Institute of Technology independently developed the iRoadSafe smart road safety warning system. This technology is a solution that integrates communication, sensors, traffic signs, facilities and other information, and calculates the distance between people, vehicles and objects through anti-collision algorithms to further predict the trajectory and issue warning messages.
In other words, sensors such as radar and light-distance are installed at intersections to detect human, vehicle and material information. Through vehicle-mounted short-range wireless communication technology (DSRC), vehicles can communicate directly with vehicles and vehicles to road, breaking visual blind spot obstacles, and using electronic boards to remind passers-by to achieve safety and anti-collision effects.Although current cars can detect whether there are objects in front of them through the vehicle radar for braking warning, car radars often cause blind spots to detect the intersection due to buildings or other vehicles at intersections. At this time, it is necessary to rely on the radar or light sensor on the road to detect the intersection dynamics from a high point to expand the driver's protection range.
This technology has been applied to the anti-collision warning system for the intersections of buses and Kaohsiung Light Rail in Ren'ai Road in Taipei City. New Taipei City has also conducted demonstration applications for intersections with large traffic flow. Taichung has set up anti-collision warning systems for intersections that are prone to accidents. Some intersections in other six cities such as Taoyuan and Tainan have related applications.This technology has also been transferred to domestic automotive communication manufacturers, including Yaxun Technology, Dinghan International, Fuhua Electronics, etc.
