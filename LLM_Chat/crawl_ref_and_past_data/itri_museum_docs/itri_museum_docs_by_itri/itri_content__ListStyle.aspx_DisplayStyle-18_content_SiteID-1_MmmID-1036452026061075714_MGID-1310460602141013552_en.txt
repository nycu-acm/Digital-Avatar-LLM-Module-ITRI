Industry Focus Spotlights
Insight into the trends and competitiveness of the global semiconductor industry in 2025
Written by Zou Ming-chan
AI-driven, packaging breakout, new semiconductor game
The Institute of Technology held a seminar to share global semiconductor trends. In the face of uncertain trade situations and technological control risks, industries need to improve linkage, flexibly deploy diversified markets, and increase competitiveness.
The global semiconductor market continues to expand, and Taiwan's semiconductor output value has risen accordingly, and the application of AI and high-efficiency computing has become the key growth momentum.However, with the rapid evolution of technology and the interweaving of geopolitical changes, the industry faces the challenges of structural reshaping and intensifying competition. From IC design, advanced packaging to policy layout, 2025 will be a key turning point in the semiconductor industry. All sectors are accelerating their layout and striving to gain advantages in the changing situation.
As global inflation pressure gradually eases, terminal market buying has recovered. In 2024, the global semiconductor industry ushered in a strong rebound, with the market size reaching US$630.5 billion and an annual growth rate of 19.7%, which is better than expected.According to the World Semiconductor Trade Statistics Association (WSTS), the global semiconductor output value will rise to US$700.9 billion in 2025, with an annual growth rate of 11.2%.
In this wave of growth, AI and high-efficiency computing (HPC) are still the main driving forces, while Taiwan's semiconductor industry performed more impressively, with output value exceeding NT$5.3 trillion in 2024 and is estimated to reach NT$6.3 trillion in 2025, once again demonstrating Taiwan's stable competitive strength in the global supply chain.
AI continues to drive semiconductor kinetic energy
Fan Zhehao, manager of the Institute of Industrial Technology International Strategy Development of the Institute of Industrial Research Institute, pointed out that driven by generative AI, semiconductor applications and process technologies are evolving rapidly.From AI chips, memory, high-speed data transmission to advanced packaging, related technical fields have significantly heated up.
Taking AI processors as an example, the growth rate in 2024 is as high as 219%.According to the estimates of international research institution Gartner, it will continue to maintain rapid growth in 2025. With the increasing demand for high-speed transmission and low latency in data centers, solutions for emerging technologies such as co-package optics (CPO) and high-bandwidth memory (HBM) are emerging rapidly.
At the same time, changes in global political and economic situations have also reshaped the appearance of the supply chain.Fan Zhehao pointed out that from the memory super cycle in 2017 and the accelerated digital transformation of the epidemic in 2021 to the geopolitical impact in recent years, the industrial model is gradually shifting from the professional division of labor that pursues cost-effectiveness to focusing on local manufacturing and regional balanced layout.How to master growth opportunities in the AI ​​wave and consolidate its advantages under technology and regional competition will be a key issue in the semiconductor industry in 2025.
AI technology accelerates its implementation, chip design is integrated into cloud
AI applications are rapidly implemented, which is greatly changing the development pattern of the IC design industry, gradually extending from the data center to edge and terminal devices.Wang Xuanzhi, an industry analyst at the Institute of Obstetrics and International, Industrial Research Institute, pointed out that the global semiconductor design market is expected to increase by 29.5% year-on-year in 2024, of which the annual growth rate of AI processors is as high as 219%.
Chip design has also accelerated its transformation in response to the evolution of AI models.Large AI models need to have pre-training and inference capabilities that support the "Scaling Law" to promote the rapid development of high computing power and high bandwidth memory.For example, the NVIDIA Rubin platform uses HBM4 memory and TSMC's COUPE and SoIC-X advanced packaging technology to promote AI computing power from vertical expansion to horizontal extension.
AI model optimization technology, such as quantization, pruning and knowledge distillation, enables small and medium-sized models to significantly reduce the demand for computing resources while maintaining inference efficiency, showing performance close to that of large models.With the chip design of lightweight and high-energy-efficient architectures, AI can be deployed on resource-constrained edge devices to achieve low power consumption and low latency inference capabilities, effectively responding to users' high demand for immediacy and privacy protection.
On the platform side, AI deployment is also accelerated simultaneously. NVIDIA launches personal inference platforms such as DGX Spark and DGX Station, supporting 200 billion to megaparameter models; on the personal and enterprise side, Intel, AMD and Qualcomm improve the product line of AI personal computers; the new generation of professional AI graphics cards significantly improve memory and computing efficiency, meeting diverse application needs.
In addition, the rise of new devices such as smart glasses has also promoted the upgrading of terminal chip technology.MediaTek and Qualcomm have launched mobile phone chips that support multimodal models, bringing more powerful AI computing power to smart devices.Wang Xuanzhi pointed out that AI technology and applications are accelerating the integration, driving IC design from a single application scenario to a cross-platform and cross-carrier integration layout, and also opening a new strategic vision for Taiwanese IC design operators.
Silicon photon co-encapsulation optics move towards commercialization
Silicon photon co-encapsulation optical technology, which has the advantages of high bandwidth, low power consumption and heterogeneity integration, has become a potential solution to support the AI ​​supercomputing architecture.However, moving towards commercial use still faces several technical challenges.Zhang Yunyi, an industry analyst at the Institute of Obstetrics and International, Industrial Research Institute, pointed out that the three major topics of "parallel accuracy", "component heat dissipation" and "test process collaboration" are the key thresholds for moving from laboratory technology to mass production application.
First, the submicron precision alignment between the optical fiber or waveguide and the wafer must be achieved during the packaging stage, and a slight error may lead to signal loss.Therefore, the industry has introduced high fault-tolerant designs such as inverse conical waveguides and grating arrays, and used automated six-axis alignment equipment to improve assembly accuracy.
Secondly, component heat dissipation is also an important challenge in the practical application of co-packaging optical introduction.When optical components and wafers are packaged together, the power density will be greatly improved. If the heat dissipation is insufficient, it may affect the life and performance of the laser element.To this end, the combination of cold plates and metal thermally conductive materials has become a solution, and some businesses have also developed local liquid cooling technology to accurately control hot spots.
Third, the packaging and testing process needs to be highly integrated. Compared with traditional pluggable modules, silicon photonic modules must complete more complex photoelectric testing before packaging to reduce the risk and cost of packaging failure. The industry needs to introduce a high-density test platform and electro-optical hybrid testing architecture, and promote the standardization of test specifications.
Despite the challenges, Silicon Photon has been at a critical turning point in mass production applications.Zhang Yunyi pointed out that from NVIDIA, Intel to international manufacturers such as Marvell and Broadcom, they are actively optimizing packaging and optical engine design, and investing in mass production platform construction and testing automation; Taiwan promotes the common platform of silicon photonic alliance and heterogeneous integration through industry-university-research cooperation, and improves the local ecological system.
Overall, silicon photonic co-packaging optics is moving from R&D to mass production. The supply chain integration and process upgrades they connect will become the key pillars driving high-efficiency and low-power computing in the AI ​​era.
Policy reshapes the global territory, and corporate links become the key
Driven by the continued warming of geopolitical pressure and AI applications and technology, the global semiconductor supply chain is showing a high restructuring trend.The United States, Europe and Japan continue to attract manufacturing to implement through semiconductor policies, not only increasing regional manufacturing energy, but also bringing new variables to the global semiconductor layout.Li Jiazhen, an industry analyst at the Institute of Obstetrics and International, Institute of Industrial Research Institute, said that semiconductor policies are continuing to strengthen regional resilience and are the main driving force for countries to promote semiconductor manufacturing independence.
When the US chip and science bill was transferred at the end of last year, it approved huge subsidies from key manufacturers such as TSMC, Intel, and Micron, and laid out advanced process and packaging production bases in Arizona, Ohio and Texas.Local manufacturing of semiconductors is an important goal of the United States, so the United States may continue to use other policy tools to promote semiconductor manufacturers to invest in the United States.In Europe, the EU continues to implement the European chip bill to subsidize major manufacturers to establish production capacity in Europe and strengthen their R&D capabilities.Recently, the EU has also reviewed and evaluated the effectiveness of relevant measures, hoping to follow-up corrections to be closer to the current local industry needs.Japan recently launched a new policy framework to establish a more stable and predictable policy environment, and at the same time, it has exerted the government's fiscal leverage effect through amendments to attract more private investment to participate in related semiconductor projects.
On the other hand, Southeast Asia and India have the advantages of risk diversification and adjacent terminal markets, quickly become the first choice for international corporate supply chain reconstruction.TI, Micron, ASE and other companies are actively expanding packaging and testing facilities in Malaysia, Singapore and the Philippines, which not only corresponds to geopolitical risks, but also strengthens supply chain flexibility and cost competitiveness.
Overall, policy-driven is accelerating the regional development of the global semiconductor industry, and simultaneously testing enterprises' ability to integrate upstream and downstream of the supply chain.In the face of uncertain trade situations and technological control risks, only by improving linkage and flexibly deploying diversified markets can manufacturers stabilize their own advantages between global competition and the wave of regionalization.
