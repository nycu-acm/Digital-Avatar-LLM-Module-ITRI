Technology introduction
Continuous microwave-assisted thermal cracking equipment.
Chemical regeneration is a recycling technology that converts organic polymers into small molecules through chemical reactions and re-applied.The Institute of Technology has developed continuous waste plastic microwave thermal cracking chemical regeneration technology, which assists the operation of high-efficiency cracking process through microwave heating, converts waste into economical petrochemical raw materials and recycles it, reducing the large amount of carbon emissions caused by incineration treatment.Taking polystyrene (PolyStyrene; PS) as an example, it can be decomposed into styrene (Styrene Monomer; SM) for recycling and recycled to existing industrial chains, which will help produce recycled products that meet international regulations and brand requirements, making the operators more competitive in the supply of international low-carbon recycling raw materials.
Features and innovation
The continuous waste plastic microwave thermal cracking process can introduce innovatively designed multi-effect catalysts to induce reaction pathways and improve the target product selection rate; at the same time, weaken the reaction activation energy, which helps reduce processing temperature and reduce process energy consumption.The facility system combines high-efficiency microwave heating units and continuous screw reactors, and is equipped with a multi-effect catalyst and a steam heat exchange unit that is renewable and circulates. It has the technical advantages of rapid heating and short steam retention time. It converts waste plastic that is not easy to physically recover into liquid oil, and then can produce various valuable low-carbon raw materials and be recycled after proper separation.
Application and benefits
1. Recyclable circulating raw materials.
2. Reduce carbon emissions at the process end.
3. Reduce and add value of agricultural, forestry and plastic waste.
