Technology introduction
Synchronization technology of supercritical fluid and dyeing.
The circular economy is a trend, not only the development focus of the "5+2 Industrial Innovation Plan", but also regarded as the decisive key to Taiwan's transformation of the global industrial chain.The Institute of Technology has developed the "supercritical fluid dyeing and functionalization synchronization technology" that "no water use", filling the industrial gap from the material end and the technical end, and opening a smooth road to the blue ocean market for Taiwan's textile industry.
No water is needed for fabric dyeing?In the laboratory of the Institute of Technology, a steel furnace up to 10 cm thick is filled with carbon dioxide that reaches the supercritical gas-liquid state. The temperature in the furnace is as high as 120 degrees Celsius and the pressure reaches 250 Bar. The laboratory outside the boiler is also so hot that it makes people sweat profusely.Put the fabric into the steel furnace, open the furnace door after 4 hours, and rolls of polyester fiber and elastic fabric have been dyed with bright colors. There is no need for a drop of water during the dyeing process, and it can also give the fabric functions such as moisture absorption and sweating. This is the magic of the "simultaneous technology of supercritical fluid dyeing and functionalization" of the Institute of Technology Research.
Over the past four years, the R&D team of the Institute of Technology has repeatedly conducted thousands of experiments and developed a variety of "supercritical fluid staining and functionalization synchronization technologies (The Supercritical CO)

Features and innovation
The textile industry is an important industry in Taiwan, with an annual export amount of 10 billion US dollars. Many international brands come to Taiwan to purchase. However, in recent years, environmental awareness has risen. International sports brands have called for the establishment of the "Zero Emissions of Hazardous Chemicals" (ZDHC) to formulate strict standards for zero emissions of hazardous chemicals by 2020. After the Paris Agreement was signed by countries around the world in 2015, European and American companies have become increasingly strict in the carbon emission standards of trade products, which is a arduous challenge for the traditional dyeing and finishing industry that produces a large amount of wastewater during the production process.
"In the past 10 years, we have fully felt the anxiety of textile industry players," said Tang Jingwen, head of the Fiber and Textile Chemicals Technology Group of the Institute of Materials and Chemical Engineering of the Institute of Technology. In traditional dyeing procedures, a large amount of water is required for dyeing and washing.According to statistics, 25 liters of water will be used for every piece of clothing manufactured, which is equivalent to the capacity of 15 large bottles of special bottles.Therefore, all the operators are working hard to reduce wastewater discharge and meet world environmental standards.
Since 2014, large textile factories in Taiwan have built supercritical fluid dyeing equipment for water-free dyeing. With few dye types, they have invested a lot of resources to develop process parameters in order to smoothly introduce them into mass production.Dong Minyan, director of the Textile and People's Livelihood Chemical Research Office of the Institute of Materials and Chemical Engineering, Industrial Research Institute, found that although there were supercritical dyeing equipment on the market at that time, there were almost no dyeing parameters research. Therefore, he began to develop high-definition fastness dyes with red, yellow and blue primary colors, so that the water-free dyeing fabrics can have richer color changes and can firmly lock in the color. After verification by cooperative manufacturers, it was found that the quality was not inferior to traditional products at all.Later, the supercritical fluid dyeing open innovation system platform (OISP) was gradually created through the cross-unit cooperation of the Institute of Industrial Research and Research, industry-university-research and international cooperation models, and quickly increased the energy of independent research and development.
The biggest feature of supercritical fluid dyeing and functionalization synchronization technology is that during the dyeing process, carbon dioxide is replaced by water, and a special boiler is used to apply a specific temperature and pressure to allow carbon dioxide to reach a supercritical state between gas and liquid. At the same time, it has the high solubility of liquid and the high penetration of gas, so that the dye can be dissolved and diffused and completely penetrated into the fiber capillary pores.The used carbon dioxide can be recycled and reused through the air valve, and there is no need to add a drying procedure like traditional dyeing and finishing technology to increase waste gas pollution.
Application and benefits
The synchronization technology of supercritical fluid dyeing and functionalization can be only applied to polyester fiber fabrics, and polyester fiber is the most common material of functional fabrics. Generally, functional fabric dyeing and adding functions are two separate processes. Dong Minyan had an idea during the discussion with the industry and began to try to develop synchronous dyeing and functionalization technologies.
However, because this technology is the first in the world and there is no precedent for reference, Dong Minyan and his team used the supercritical dyeing OISP platform to establish a digital gene database (MGI/AI) to assist in the simulation to determine which materials to be used for synthesis, and then send the synthetic dye/functionalized materials to the supercritical dye verification process.The R&D team established hundreds of material information from scratch and conducted no less than a thousand experiments, which established a complete verification mechanism, which can quickly and flexibly find the most suitable materials for the operators.
"The demand of manufacturers is the driving force for our R&D," said Dong Minyan. While he was working hard to find resources, the cooperative manufacturers also actively responded to the actual use needs, so that the R&D team could better hit the industrial pain points; its international brand customers also attached great importance to this technology, making him more sure that this technology will be the trend in the future.After all, on the road to pursuing environmental sustainability, technology and innovation must be the best partners.
Technical highlights: The dyeing and functionalization of polyester fiber and elastic fabrics can be completed simultaneously without a drop of water.The carbon dioxide used for dyeing can also be recycled and recycled, which truly conforms to the concept of sustainable circular economy.
