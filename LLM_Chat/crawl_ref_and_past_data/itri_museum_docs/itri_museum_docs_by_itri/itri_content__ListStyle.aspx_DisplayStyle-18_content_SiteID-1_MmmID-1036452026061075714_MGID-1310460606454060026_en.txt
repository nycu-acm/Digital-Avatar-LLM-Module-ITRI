AI chips that integrate soft and hard, save more than ten times the power
Written by Chen Yiru
Open a new edge computing situation
The Institute of Technology adopts "In-Memory Computing" (CIM) technology, allowing AI chips to operate for a long time, improve performance, and have ultra-low power consumption.
The AI ​​wave has brought revolutionary innovation, and terminal devices have increasingly high requirements for computing efficiency and power saving.The Institute of Technology has broken through the technical bottleneck and created the "Ultra-low-power edge recognition of next-generation computing architecture integrated with soft and hard integration" technology with ultra-low power consumption and elastic configuration, allowing AI computing to save more than 10 times the power, set a world record in energy efficiency, and won the Gold Medal of Outstanding Research Award.
Looking at robots, drones, sensors, wearable devices, smart cameras and other devices, AI applications are everywhere in the future, but these terminal edge devices often face technical bottlenecks such as high power consumption, slow speed, and huge and complex AI algorithms.In order to break through the limitations, the Institute of Technology has used soft and hard integration technology, combined with its own developed AI model and circuit architecture, to create the "ultra-low-power edge recognition technology for the integration of soft and hard integration of next-generation computing architectures" technology, and successfully developed AI chips that can save more than 10 times the power, allowing AI operations that could only be performed in cloud or advanced computers in the past, but can now be implemented in small and extremely power-saving edge devices, which will help AI applications to blossom in full.
"Only other AI chips can do this architecture!" Xu Shixuan, head of the Institute of Electronics and Optoelectronics Systems of the Institute of Industrial Research Institute, said that the key to saving more than 10 times of power is to use "Computing in Memory" (CIM) technology. Traditional computing architectures need to continuously transfer data between the processor and the memory, which consumes time and energy; CIM saves frequent transfer of data, and can also support high-parallelometric computing, greatly saving computing time and power consumption.
Optimize the integration of soft and hard and stuff the brain into the chip
In fact, many teams around the world have invested in CIM research, but the unique feature of this technology developed by the Institute of Technology is that it is a dual-pronged software and hardware integration, realizing a truly ground-based, long-term operation and high-performance ultra-low-power AI chip, as if it is stuffing the AI ​​brain into a chip the size of a fingertip.
In terms of hardware design, the circuit structure adopts an extremely low voltage design, reducing the process voltage of generally 1 to 1.2 volts to below 0.4 volts, greatly reducing computing power consumption.However, although extremely low voltage can save power consumption, the biggest problem is that electrical deviation will occur during operation, such as the current from the original 1 ampere to 1.1 or 0.9 ampere. "If you use a low voltage but there is no trick to resist electrical deviation, the calculation results may be wrong," said Li Sihan, deputy head of the R&D of the Institute of Electrical and Optical Systems of the Institute of Industrial Research Institute.Therefore, the team added an anti-offset mechanism to the circuit design, allowing CIM to operate at low voltage and achieve stable computing.
In terms of software design, it is optimized for matrix multiplication and addition operations (MACs) that account for more than 70% to 80% of AI operations, focusing on high-intensity operations at the Neural Network (NN) level, and simplifying the pre-processing process. In addition, the team also performed "task equivalent" according to the application scenario.Xu Shijie explained that the data accuracy should be moderately reduced, only the most necessary information should be retained, the calculation burden should be reduced, and energy efficiency should be improved.For example, a robot only needs to judge the outline of the object and does not need to identify color details, which greatly reduces the amount of calculation without affecting the accuracy of recognition.
"The more streamlined the AI ​​model, the smaller the hardware burden, and the higher the energy efficiency," Li Sihan explained that this AI chip architecture can be flexibly adjusted according to the application. Whether it is small tasks detected by sensors or high computing needs such as image analysis, they can "increase" or "slim down" according to requirements. It not only supports micro AI cores, but also connects to multiple CIM accelerators, which are extremely elastic.
Energy efficiency sets world record, helping AI applications flourish
The team is composed of 20 software and hardware experts, constantly optimizing and iterating. Currently, the energy efficiency performance of 1-bit and 4-bit operations has set world records.Among them, the 1-bit operation performance reaches more than 20,000 TOPS/W per watt (20,943 TOPS/W), far exceeding the current average of about 1,000 TOPS/W.In fact, many chips have low voltage technology, but the one who imports it into CIM and maintains world records is the team of the Industrial Research Institute.
In addition to its brilliant technical performance, the team has also published 18 international journals and conference papers. From 2020 to 2025, it was selected as the "International Solid State Circuit Conference" (ISSCC), a top conference known as the "Olympic of the chip industry", for six consecutive years, fully demonstrating the leading position of the Institute of Technology in CIM technology.
At present, this technology has cooperated with semiconductor and smart sensor manufacturers to complete low-power voice and video verification. In the future, it will first enter niche markets such as smart manufacturing, energy monitoring and medical wear, assist customers in developing new products with market potential and gradually expanding their application landscape.
Xu Shixuan specifically pointed out that drones and robots will be the next wave of application focus. "In the future, every household will have robots, and each unit will have hundreds of sensors, and the same number of AI chips are needed to process data." What these terminal devices need most is "power-saving, flexible and integrated" AI chips that can support huge demand for real-time AI computing.
This technology combines three advantages: architectural innovation, energy efficiency breakthrough and flexible application. It not only sets a global record, but also successfully integrates with the industry and has high potential for implementation.Because of this, the team also won the 2025 Outstanding Research Gold Medal, which is well deserved, setting an important milestone for Taiwan in the fields of AI chip design and edge computing.
