Memory
In the 1980s, Taiwan did not have the technology to make memory. The DRAM used by the operators were imported from abroad. Around 1986, the global DRAM was out of stock, which also led to Taiwanese computer operators being unable to ship or could only tolerate soaring prices.At that time, memory accounted for 25% of the cost of computer manufacturing. In order to ensure that the source of DRAM is safe, in 1990, the Institute of Technology launched the "Sub-micro Process Technology Development Plan" and recruited Dr. Lu Zhiyuan, who was working at Bell Laboratory at the time, to establish an eight-inch wafer sub-micrometer laboratory, with the goal of increasing Taiwan's IC manufacturing capacity from 1 micron at that time to 0.5 micron.
In 1993, the first batch of 8-inch wafer 0.5mm 16Mb DRAM verification was successfully carried out, making it the first DRAM product produced by Chinese people with their own technology.In 1994, the first "World Advanced Integrated Circuit Company" in Taiwan with the strength of R&D and mass production of DRAM was derived.The sub-micron project not only enables Taiwan to have independent DRAM technology, drives the investment boom of eight-inch wafer manufacturers, but also cultivates more than 300 engineers with outstanding R&D capabilities, leading Taiwan's semiconductor industry to its peak.So far, with the accelerated development of AI artificial intelligence, 5G and AIoT, the Industrial Research Institute has continued to develop the next generation of forward-looking memory, paving the way for Taiwanese factories to advance the new generation of memory.
