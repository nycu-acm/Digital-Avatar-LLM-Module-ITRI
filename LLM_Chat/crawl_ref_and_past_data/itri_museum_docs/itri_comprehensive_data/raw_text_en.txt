Since its establishment in 1973, it has taken the lead in investing in the research and development of integrated circuits and nurtured emerging technology industries; it has accumulated nearly 30,000 patents, and has newly created and cultivated listed cabinet companies including TSMC, UMC, Jingyuan Optoelectronics, etc., to drive industrial development. It is headquartered in Hsinchu, Taiwan, and has offices in the United States, Europe and Japan. Introduction [Editor] Industrial Research Institute is Taiwan's largest industrial technology research and development institution and a pioneer in the creation of Taiwan's semiconductor industry [1]. At the end of 1969, Sun Yun-chan, who had just taken over as Minister of Economic Affairs, was invited to visit South Korea. When visiting the Institute of Science and Technology in Seoul, South Korea, he was impressed by the Korean government's approach to giving preferential treatment and flexible regulations to scientific and technological talents to develop the technology industry. After returning to the country, Sun Yun-chan, then Minister of Economic Affairs, promoted the merger of the United Industrial Research Institute, the United Mining Institute, and the Institute of Metal Industry, to establish the Institute of Industrial Technology, and gave the Institute of Industrial Technology to promote Taiwan's industrial development and economic takeoff with advanced industrial technology. In 1975, RCA was selected as a partner in Taiwan's integrated circuit cooperation project. In 1976, the first batch of engineers who went to the United States to train were ready to go. In 1977, the Institute of Industrial Technology established Taiwan's first 4-inch wafer integrated circuit demonstration factory, and since 1980, it has successively derived UMC, TSMC, and Taiwan photomonitors. , world-advanced semiconductor manufacturers laid the foundation for the takeoff of Taiwan's IC industry; in 1983, the Institute of Industrial Research and Research also developed personal computers compatible with IBM, and transferred technology to domestic operators, driving the vigorous development of surrounding industries and laying the foundation for the personal computer information industry. The Institute of Industrial Research and Research has used the uniqueness of the R&D environment and connected the R&D energy of the region or industry through a dense and solid industrial network relationship to actively assist Taiwan's industrial development. Since 2005, in order to cooperate with Taiwan's overall regional industrial development policy and strengthen the connection between science and technology and local industries, the Institute of Industrial Technology has successively established the "South Branch" in Luojia, Tainan and the "South Branch" in Nantou, and deeply cultivated the R&D zone for Taiwan's industrial innovation.
